What Composing Music Taught Me About Building a Business

Tyrone Showers writes and performs music alongside running Taliferro, and the two turn out to draw on the same instincts more often than people expect — vision, discipline, and knowing when a piece is actually finished.

Published: 17 Aug 2023 · Updated: 6 Sep 2026

Blog home Browse topics

By Tyrone Showers

Co-Founder Taliferro

Article

Introduction

I write and perform music alongside running a business, and the overlap between the two shows up more often than I expected going in. Here's where composing and running Taliferro actually draw on the same muscle.

Branding Is a Composition

Building a brand and writing a piece of music both come down to the same task: picking a small set of elements — colors, tone, a melodic idea — and making sure they all point in the same direction long enough for someone else to recognize it.

Product Design Needs the Same Instinct as Composing

A product that works and a piece of music that lands share the same tension: function has to serve the experience, not fight it. When a design gets that balance right, it produces the same kind of quiet satisfaction as a well-resolved musical phrase.

Why Companies Invest in Art and Music

Companies that back art and sponsor music aren't just doing PR — they're tapping into something real about how people connect with a brand emotionally, not just transactionally. Having stood on both sides of that relationship, as an artist and as a founder, I've seen how much that connection is worth when it's genuine.

The Business Side of Making Art

Making a living from art or music isn't just creative work — it requires the same market awareness, pricing judgment, and audience-building that any other business does. Nobody warns you about that part going in.

Entrepreneurship Runs on the Same Instincts as Composing

Starting a company and writing a piece of music both demand a vision for something that doesn't exist yet, the willingness to take a real risk on it, and the discipline to keep refining it past the point where it's comfortable. That overlap is why creative people often make surprisingly good founders.

Conclusion

For me, art and business were never really separate disciplines — they're two applications of the same underlying skill: taking a rough idea, refining it, and shipping something that resonates with someone else.

Working across both hasn't diluted either one. If anything, composing has made me a sharper founder, and running a company has made me a more disciplined composer.

Tyrone Showers
