Experience Is What Turns Time Into Judgment

Two people can log the same number of years and come away with very different judgment — the difference is what they actually did with the time. Taliferro looks at how time and experience actually build on each other.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Aug 9, 2023 · 2 min read

Introduction

Time is the one resource nobody gets more of, but it doesn't automatically produce wisdom on its own — it has to be converted into experience first, and experience is what actually feeds back into how time gets spent next. Here's how that loop actually works, and why it matters for personal growth.

Time Doesn't Come Back

Once time is spent, it's gone — there's no getting it back, which is exactly why how it gets allocated matters so much:

  • Goal achievement: what gets done depends directly on where time actually goes, not where it was intended to go.
  • Relationships: time spent with people is what builds the relationship — attention is the actual currency.
  • Skill building: time invested in learning is what turns into real professional capability later.

Experience Is Time With the Lessons Extracted

Experience is what's left after time has been spent and something was learned from it — encounters, choices, failures, and wins. That extraction shows up as:

  • Judgment: both good and bad experiences sharpen the instinct behind future decisions.
  • Empathy: a wider range of experience makes it easier to actually understand other people's situations.
  • Resilience: getting through hard experiences is what builds the capacity to handle the next one.

How Time and Experience Feed Each Other

The relationship runs in both directions:

  1. Time produces experience — time spent on a hobby, career, travel, or relationships is what generates something to learn from in the first place.
  2. Professional growth: time spent in education and work is what compounds into real expertise.
  3. Relationships: time spent with family and friends is what turns into connection worth having.
  4. Experience then redirects time — the lessons from past experience change how future time gets allocated.
  5. Better decisions: past mistakes and wins are what inform smarter choices going forward.
  6. Clearer priorities: experience is what teaches which things are actually worth the time.

Conclusion

Time by itself doesn't teach anything — it has to be spent on something and reflected on before it turns into experience. That experience is what then determines whether the next stretch of time gets spent more wisely or gets wasted the same way as the last one.

That loop is worth paying attention to on purpose: what gets done with time now is what determines the quality of judgment available later, when the decisions actually matter more.
