Work doesn't explode It loosens

Someone says they'll handle it. A note gets buried in a thread. The follow-up slips two days. None of those moments look like failure on their own — and that's exactly what makes execution drift dangerous. It's cumulative, not sudden, and by the time it's visible, the momentum is already gone.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Jul 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Execution drift is one of those problems that almost never announces itself clearly.

Work does not explode. It loosens. Context fades. Owners become less obvious. Deadlines soften. The next move gets harder to see.

That is why Taliferro cares so much about surfaces like Moves, Network, and Outreach. They exist to keep the next action and the surrounding context from dissolving between conversations.

Execution drift usually looks normal

That is what makes it dangerous.

  • someone says they will handle it
  • a note gets buried in a thread
  • the follow-up gets delayed by two days
  • the reply no longer matches the original context
  • the team assumes someone else moved it forward

None of those moments look like failure on their own. Together they create a real slowdown.

Why follow-through is the counterweight

Better follow-through does not just mean “working harder.” It means creating enough visibility that the next move survives the day.

When follow-through is reliable, work stays anchored. The promise is easier to trace. The owner is clearer. The conversation does not have to be rebuilt every time it resumes.

What better follow-through actually means

  • the next action is captured while it is still clear
  • the context remains attached to the action
  • the team can see what needs attention next
  • the system reduces the need to remember everything manually

That is why follow-through is not an etiquette issue. It is an operational issue. It determines whether the organization acts like it remembers its own momentum.

FAQ

What is execution drift?

Execution drift is the slow erosion of clarity, ownership, and urgency that happens after a decision is made and before the promised action actually happens.

Why is execution drift hard to notice?

Because none of the individual moments, like a delayed reply or a buried note, look like failure on their own. The damage is cumulative, not sudden.

What does better follow-through actually require?

Capturing the next action while it is still clear, keeping context attached to it, and reducing reliance on memory to carry work forward.

How does Taliferro address execution drift?

Surfaces like Moves, Network, and Outreach are built to keep the next action and its context visible between conversations instead of letting it dissolve.
