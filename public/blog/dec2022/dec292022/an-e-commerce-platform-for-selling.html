An E-Commerce Platform Doesn't Sell A Checkout People Finish Does

A beautiful storefront with a checkout people abandon isn't a sales channel — it's a very expensive brochure. Most e-commerce projects spend their budget on how the store looks and almost nothing on the four or five steps between "add to cart" and "paid," which is exactly where most of the money quietly leaks out.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Dec 29, 2022 · 1 min read

The Storefront Gets the Attention

Most e-commerce projects spend almost all their budget and review time on the home page, the product photos, and the brand feel. That work matters, but it isn't what decides whether the platform sells anything. A visitor who likes what they see and still abandons the cart never shows up in a "the site looks great" conversation — they just leave, and the business never finds out why.

When the deeper issue is stalled execution, system design that removes drag shows how Taliferro turns execution work into working execution, and the execution-first operating model keeps the work tied to outcomes instead of activity.

Where Checkouts Actually Lose People

An account requirement before someone can see the total cost. Shipping and tax that only appear on the final screen instead of upfront. A form that resets when one field fails validation. None of these look like bugs in a demo — they only show up as a drop in completed orders, which is why they survive so many launches unnoticed.

Product availability and shipping cost are the two questions almost every buyer asks before paying. If the platform makes them guess, or answers late in the flow, some percentage of otherwise-ready buyers close the tab right there.

What a Checkout People Finish Looks Like

Total cost, including shipping and tax, visible before the final step. A guest checkout option, not a forced account. Clear, honest delivery timing instead of a vague "processing time" note. None of this is exciting to design. It is, however, the part of an e-commerce platform that actually determines revenue, which is why Taliferro treats it as core scope, not a late-stage polish item.

The Real Metric

A store isn't measured by how many people visit. It's measured by how many people who wanted to buy something actually finished buying it. That number lives in the checkout, not the homepage.
