The Real Reason to Outsource IT Isn't Cost — It's Attention

Outsourcing IT usually gets pitched as a cost play, and sometimes it is one. But the bigger shift is what a founder or ops lead stops thinking about — patching, vendor calls, the 2am server alert — and starts spending that attention on instead. Taliferro has watched that trade play out with clients more times than the cost savings alone would explain.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Feb 9, 2023 · 2 min read

Where the Time Actually Goes

Most small businesses don't lack IT skills so much as they lack IT attention. Someone — often the founder — is the de facto IT department, fielding password resets and vendor renewals between the work they were actually supposed to be doing. Delegating the technology function to an outside team isn't just cheaper in a lot of cases — it's the fastest way to get that attention back.

What You Actually Get Back

If you're like most small businesses, your top priority is growing the company and serving customers well — not keeping up with patch cycles. With an outsourced IT function in place, someone else handles staying current on new technology and fixing what breaks, which means the people who used to be pulled into tech support can go back to the work that actually moves the business: sales, marketing, product.

Before outsourcing, it's worth checking two things about a potential partner: whether they've actually run projects like yours before, and whether the specific work you need (server migrations, day-to-day helpdesk, security) is something they specialize in rather than something they'll figure out as they go.

Reduced Costs

Outsourcing your IT department will reduce your costs in the following ways:

  • Cost of hiring and training new staff. You no longer have to worry about paying for new employees' salaries, health insurance, and other benefits when you outsource your IT department. You also won't have to train them on the company's systems or software. Most outsourced companies already have their employees trained in specific technologies and can begin work right away when they arrive at your office or business location. They'll often come with references from other clients who were satisfied with their services too.
  • Cost of maintaining infrastructure — servers, licenses, bandwidth. Outsourced providers are already set up to keep that running without it becoming its own project, and it's bundled into what you're already paying instead of showing up as a surprise line item.
  • Cost of managing security issues — malware, ransomware, and the ongoing work of keeping sensitive customer data actually protected instead of just insured against.

Greater Access to Skill Sets

A key benefit of outsourcing your IT department is that you can hire people with specific skills, even if they are unavailable in your country or region. For example, suppose the best developers live in Silicon Valley, but you're based in Europe. In that case, you can outsource all your development work to them without relocating yourself or anyone else. This gives companies access to a broader pool of talent, at a lower cost than trying to recruit and relocate that same expertise in-house.

It also tends to mean better security and scalability, since a provider who does this full-time has already dealt with the failure modes a small business would otherwise learn the hard way.

The Takeaway

The cost savings from outsourcing IT are real, but they're not the interesting part. The interesting part is what your team stops spending its attention on — and what it starts spending that attention on instead. That trade is worth running the numbers on regardless of company size.
