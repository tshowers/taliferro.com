Your CRM Shouldn't Just Store Contacts — It Should Tell You Who to Call Today

Most CRMs are good at storage and bad at prompting action — they hold onto contact information without ever surfacing who's stalling, who's at risk, or who needs a follow-up today. A relationship system should point you at the next move, not just remember the last one.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Feb 8, 2023 · 2 min read

Introduction

Most CRMs are good at storage and bad at prompting action — they hold contact information without ever surfacing who's stalling, who's at risk, or who needs a follow-up today. Strong customer relationships depend on the second part, not the first.

When the deeper issue is stalled execution, system design that removes drag shows how Taliferro turns execution work into working execution, and the Momentum System keeps the work tied to outcomes instead of activity.

What Actually Moves the Needle

Relationship building is about tracking every touchpoint, not just sending a thank-you note. Here's what actually works:

  • Map the journey, don't guess it. Lay out each stage — activation, onboarding, retention, expansion — so you can see the exact moment a customer stalls, and know where to act.
  • Score the lifecycle, not just the sale. Track metrics that matter: activation rate (who actually uses what they bought), retention (who stays), and lifetime value (who's worth your time). Without these, you're flying blind.
  • Log every channel. Customers expect to move from email to chat to phone without repeating themselves. Every call, message, and support ticket needs to be logged and visible — no more "I thought someone else handled it."
  • Automate the routine, personalize the moments that count. Automate what's repetitive — follow-up reminders, status updates — but keep a human on critical deal moments and escalations. A bot where a customer needed a person costs trust.
  • Close the loop fast. Feedback isn't a survey collected once a year. Ask after key milestones, and act on what comes back within days, not quarters.

Conclusion

Relationship-building is outcome-driven: track activation, retention, and lifetime value instead of just storing contacts. Use journey mapping to spot where customers drop off, automate the routine follow-ups, and personalize the moments that matter. If you can't say what your current retention rate is and which accounts need attention today, the system isn't working hard enough yet.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you measure relationship strength?

Track activation, retention, repeat purchase rate, referrals, NPS/CSAT, and customer lifetime value (LTV). Review these monthly and tie actions to movement.

What are quick wins for loyalty?

Faster first response, proactive status updates, consistent issue templates, and a “make it right” escalation path that’s documented and visible.

Which tools matter most?

A system that centralizes contacts, touchpoints, and follow‑ups so nothing slips. We prefer outcome‑focused systems (MS) over traditional CRMs focused only on storage.
