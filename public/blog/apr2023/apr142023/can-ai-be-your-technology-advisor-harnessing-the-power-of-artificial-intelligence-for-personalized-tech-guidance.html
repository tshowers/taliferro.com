AI Makes a Decent First Draft of a Tech Decision — Not a Final One

AI is a fast research assistant that doesn't know your team, your technical debt, or your budget unless you tell it. Taliferro uses it exactly that way internally: to accelerate the analysis, never to make the call.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Apr 14, 2023 · 1 min read

Introduction

"Can AI be my tech advisor?" gets treated as a speculative, someday question. The honest answer is narrower and less exciting: yes, for a specific slice of the job, already, today — and no, not for the part that actually requires knowing your situation.

What AI Is Actually Good At Here

AI is a fast, tireless research assistant. Ask it to compare three frameworks, list common pitfalls of a database migration, or summarize the tradeoffs between two cloud providers, and it'll pattern-match against a huge body of public knowledge faster than you could Google it yourself. That's genuinely useful — it turns a half-day of research into a ten-minute first pass.

What It Can't Know

What it can't do is know your team's actual skill level, the technical debt already sitting in your codebase, the budget you're not allowed to say out loud, or the politics of why the last migration got shelved. It'll answer confidently anyway, because generic best-practice advice is what it was trained on — and generic advice applied to a specific, messy situation is exactly how bad technical decisions get made to look reasonable in a slide deck.

How to Actually Use It

Use it to narrow the field, not to make the call. Let it generate the options and the standard tradeoffs, then have a person who actually knows your constraints — your team, a consultant, whoever's accountable for the outcome — cut that list down to what's real for your situation. It's a first draft, not a decision.

This is the same principle Taliferro applies to its own work: AI accelerates the analysis — running checks, surfacing options, flagging patterns — but strategy, architecture, and the final call stay human-led. The tools are fast. They're not accountable, and they don't know what you haven't told them.

Conclusion

Treat AI as the advisor who's read everything and knows nothing about you specifically. Great for the first pass. Never the last word.
