“We need more leads” is one of the most common requests in growth conversations.

It is also one of the least precise.

More leads is usually shorthand

When clients say they need more leads, they are often reacting to one of these problems instead:

  • the leads are weak-fit
  • the team does not follow up consistently
  • the site is attracting curiosity instead of buyers
  • the offer is not clear enough
  • nobody can tell which signals deserve attention first

This is exactly why Taliferro built pages like Lead Vault and the customer acquisition pipeline: to make the signal and the next move easier to see before more activity gets piled on top.

The real translation

A lot of the time, “we need more leads” really means:

  • we do not trust our current pipeline
  • we are tired of chasing weak demand
  • we do not know which prospects are warm
  • our follow-up is slower than we admit
  • we need a cleaner route from signal to conversation

That is an important difference. If the underlying problem is weak-fit demand or weak follow-through, adding more volume can make the mess bigger, not better.

What to measure instead

Instead of starting with volume alone, ask better questions.

  • How many leads are actually relevant?
  • How fast does the team respond?
  • Which pages or offers create the strongest buying signal?
  • Where does the conversation slow down?
  • How many leads disappear because nobody carried the next step?

Once you can answer those questions, the lead conversation gets more honest. Sometimes you do need more demand. Sometimes you need better demand. Sometimes you just need to stop losing the demand you already earned.

That is why the best client work often begins with clarification, not amplification.
