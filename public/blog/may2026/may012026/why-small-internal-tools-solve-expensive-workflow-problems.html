The smartest software decision isn't bigger software

Some of the most expensive workflow problems don't look expensive at first. They look like copy-and-paste work, search friction, a task that takes three minutes but gets repeated two hundred times a month. Individually harmless. Together, operational drag — and the fix is rarely a giant implementation.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · May 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Some of the most expensive workflow problems do not look expensive at first.

They look like copy-and-paste work. Search friction. Missing context. Repeated reformatting. Someone asking the same question every day. A task that takes three minutes but gets repeated two hundred times a month.

That is why Taliferro built smaller tools like Find, Email Signature Builder, and Ask TODD instead of pretending every problem needs a giant implementation.

The most expensive problems often look small

Individually, each interruption feels harmless. Together they become operational drag.

  • a team member loses focus finding the right file
  • an email signature takes ten tries to format correctly
  • knowledge lives in notes but is hard to reach
  • the same wording problem slows down content again and again

The money is not just in the minutes. It is in the interruption cost, the inconsistency, and the way small friction compounds into slower execution.

Why teams ignore them

Because they do not feel strategic enough to prioritize.

Leaders will approve a large initiative faster than they will fix a small repeated annoyance, even when that annoyance touches the work every single day. That is how tiny friction becomes normalized.

What a small tool does well

A good small tool does not try to own the whole company. It solves one repeated problem cleanly.

  • it reduces clicks
  • it keeps context closer to the work
  • it removes repeated formatting or lookup work
  • it saves attention, not just time

That is often enough to create a meaningful operational improvement. Sometimes the smartest software decision is not bigger software. It is one smaller tool in the right place.

FAQ

Why do small internal tools matter more than big software rollouts?

Because most operational cost comes from repeated small friction, not from missing enterprise features. A tool that removes one repeated task can pay for itself faster than a platform migration.

What makes a good internal tool?

A good small tool reduces clicks, keeps context close to the work, removes repeated formatting or lookup work, and saves attention, not just time.

What tools has Taliferro built this way?

Find, Email Signature Builder, and Ask TODD are small, focused tools Taliferro built to remove one repeated friction point instead of trying to own the whole workflow.

Do we need a big software project to fix this?

No. Most teams get more value from fixing one specific point of friction than from a general platform rollout.
