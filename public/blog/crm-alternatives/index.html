Your CRM is not broken

It is doing the wrong job

A CRM can store contacts, stages, and reports. It cannot make a forgotten follow-up happen, rescue a stalled handoff, or decide what should move next. When the system keeps giving work back to your team, the answer may not be another CRM. It may be a Momentum System.

This hub collects everything Taliferro has written about why CRMs stall, what a Momentum System actually is, and how the two compare — organized so you can jump straight to whichever part answers your question.

Quick decision checklist

  • Choose an alternative if the tool requires constant manual updates to stay accurate.
  • Fix follow-through if deals and projects die in the “we’ll get back to you” stage.
  • Reduce tool sprawl if work lives across email, docs, tasks, and chats with no owner.
  • Measure outcomes: response time, next-action coverage, completion rate, and cycle time.
  • Start small: operationalize one workflow, then scale.

The real issue

CRMs store activity. They don’t create follow-through. If your pipeline goes stale, you don’t need more fields— you need momentum.

Best next step

Why teams outgrow their CRM

Why teams outgrow their CRM

Nobody buys a CRM to create busywork. A sales leader wants order. A founder wants something sturdier than a spreadsheet. But the daily reality drifts toward data entry: a rep logs a call after it happened, updates a stage after the deal already went quiet, marks a task done after the deadline already passed. The system faithfully records what happened. It never makes anything happen.

Here's what that looks like: a prospect asks for a proposal on Tuesday. The rep says "I'll get that to you." That promise becomes a note in a stage field, not a task with an owner and a due date. By the following Tuesday, nobody remembers, the prospect has gone quiet, and the CRM still shows the deal sitting in the same stage — looking exactly as healthy as a deal that's about to close.

That's the real cost, and it's rarely on the invoice: hours spent explaining the state of work instead of doing it, and deals that go cold while the dashboard says everything is fine. See Why Your CRM Isn't Broken — It's Doing Exactly What It Was Built to Do for the longer version of this argument.

What teams are really saying

When people say the CRM is failing, they usually mean one of four things: nobody trusts the data, nobody owns the next step, too much work happens outside the system, or the team updates the record after the moment that mattered. Those are execution problems, not software complaints — and another integration won't fix them.

What replaces a CRM

What actually replaces it

"What are the best CRM alternatives" is usually the wrong question. Most teams asking it aren't looking for another database with a nicer interface — they're looking for something that reduces chasing and makes the next move obvious. That's a workflow question, not a shopping question.

A Momentum System is Taliferro's answer to it. Instead of asking "what do we know?" it asks "what should happen now?" Take that same Tuesday proposal: instead of a note in a stage field, it becomes a task with an owner and a due date the moment it's promised. If it's still open Thursday, the system surfaces it — nobody has to remember to check.

This doesn't require ripping out your CRM. Plenty of teams run a BMS before or around their CRM: the CRM keeps the records, the BMS keeps the work moving. Those are genuinely different jobs, and most CRM frustration comes from expecting one tool to do both.

What a real alternative does

  • Shows the next move without needing a meeting.
  • Reduces the amount of status-checking people do.
  • Connects outreach, tasks, documents, and ownership.
  • Improves response time and completion rate.
  • Helps teams act, not just log activity.

Start with the workflow that hurts most

Don't replace everything at once. Fix the missed follow-up, the slow proposal response, or the weak handoff first. A real alternative proves itself in one workflow before it earns the right to expand.

CRM vs BMS comparison

CRM vs. BMS, side by side

A CRM answers "what do we know?" A BMS answers "what should happen next?" A CRM tracks the object. A BMS drives the motion. That distinction matters because most teams don't actually suffer from a lack of recordkeeping — they suffer from delay, drift, missed handoffs, and scattered ownership.

Quick comparison

  • CRM: stores records, stages, notes, and reports.
  • BMS: surfaces next actions and momentum gaps.
  • CRM: depends heavily on manual upkeep.
  • BMS: reduces the distance between signal and action.
  • CRM: answers what happened.
  • BMS: helps determine what should happen now.

The simplest test

If your team spends more time updating the system than acting on what it reveals, the balance is off. If people still need a meeting to decide who owns the next move, the balance is off. See TODD vs Salesforce for what this looks like against a specific CRM.
