IT Consulting in Seattle: A Beginner’s Guide (2025)

If you've never hired an IT consulting firm before, the category itself is confusing — "IT consulting" covers everything from a single contractor fixing a network to a full team redesigning your infrastructure. Taliferro breaks down what these firms actually do, how to evaluate one, the red flags worth walking away from, and where a tool like a Momentum System fits alongside what you already have.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · May 25, 2023 · 2 min read

Introduction

"IT consulting" is a category broad enough to be nearly meaningless on its own. It covers a solo contractor who fixes printers and passwords, and it covers firms that redesign cloud infrastructure for companies with thousands of employees. If you've never hired one before, the first job is figuring out which kind you actually need — not picking a name off a search results page.

What They Actually Do

Most IT consulting firms fall into a few overlapping categories: managed IT support (day-to-day help desk, device management, network upkeep), project-based consulting (a specific migration, integration, or build), and strategic consulting (architecture decisions, technology roadmaps, security posture). A firm might do all three, or specialize in one. Knowing which category your actual problem falls into narrows the search fast — a company that needs someone to keep the network running is a different hire than one that needs a cloud migration planned.

How to Evaluate a Firm

Ask for specifics, not general reassurance. Have they done this exact type of project before, and can they name a comparable client (without breaching confidentiality)? What does their communication cadence actually look like — weekly check-ins, a shared project tracker, ad hoc emails? What happens when something goes wrong outside business hours? A firm that answers these concretely is more trustworthy than one that answers with a polished pitch deck.

Red Flags

A few patterns are worth walking away from: a firm that can't explain its pricing model clearly before work starts, one that pushes a specific vendor or platform regardless of your actual needs, or one that's vague about who on their team will actually be doing the work versus who's in the sales meeting. If every answer sounds like it's designed to close the deal rather than solve your problem, that's a signal.

Where a Momentum System Fits

Conclusion

Hiring an IT consulting firm for the first time comes down to three things: knowing which category of firm actually matches your problem, asking specific questions instead of accepting a polished pitch, and watching for the handful of red flags that predict a bad engagement. Get those right and the rest of the process is straightforward.
