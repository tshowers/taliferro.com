Introduction

Ask ten people to define leadership and you'll get ten platitudes: character, vision, leading from the front. All true enough, none of them tell you what to actually do differently tomorrow. There's a simpler, more uncomfortable test hiding underneath all of it.

The Actual Test

A leader needs followers — not people who report to them on an org chart, people who are actually following. If your team does the work the same way whether or not you say anything, and does it well because they've bought into where things are headed, that's leadership. If they only do it because you're the one who signs off, that's authority — a different thing entirely, and one that stops working the moment you're not in the room.

Know Who You're Actually Leading

Generic leadership advice fails because it isn't written for your specific team. Knowing what your people actually need — their skills, their gaps, what's genuinely motivating versus what sounds motivating in a meeting — is what lets you guide toward a real goal instead of reciting a platitude at them. A team that isn't prepared for what you're asking doesn't need inspiration; it needs the actual thing it's missing, named honestly instead of papered over.

Earned, Not Owed

A title doesn't make someone a leader, and neither does seniority, the corner office, or being first in line. Leadership is earned continuously, by doing right by the people around you, not claimed once and kept indefinitely. If the people you're supposedly leading don't actually agree that you're leading them well, that's worth sitting with honestly before stepping into the next leadership role — not something to argue past.

Conclusion

Leadership isn't power, and it isn't a title. It's whether people would still be doing the work, the same way, if you left the room. The real measure isn't how many people report to you — it's how many of them are actually better off, and actually still following, because of what you did. This is the same lens Taliferro brings into agile coaching work: teams follow leaders they trust, not org charts they're stuck under.
