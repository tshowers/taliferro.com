Our Facebook Followers Were Zero Here's What We Learned Anyway

We're a small business, and a few weeks into running our own social accounts our Facebook follower count was, honestly, zero. Most "what we learned" posts skip that part. Here's what actually happened, mistakes included.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Oct 12, 2022 · 2 min read

Introduction

A few weeks into running Taliferro's own social accounts, here's the honest scoreboard: zero followers on Facebook. Most posts about "lessons learned" skip straight to the part where it worked. Since it mostly hadn't yet, here's what we actually noticed instead.

Watch Out for Scams

Accounts claiming to represent a brand or offer help pop up constantly and look legitimate until you check. If something seems too good to be true, verify it directly with the company before clicking anything — and report it when it's fake.

Ask Course-Sellers to Show Their Actual Clients

Anyone selling a course on how to do social media should be able to point to specific campaigns and results, not just testimonials. Asking for that proof up front is itself a useful filter — the answer tells you a lot before you've spent a dollar.

Diligence Beats Talent

We weren't consistent in the first few months — posting was sporadic across Facebook, Twitter, and Instagram. What actually changed things wasn't a better strategy, it was just showing up daily instead of when we felt like it. Consistency is the unglamorous part nobody wants to hear, and it's also the part that's entirely within your control.

Zero Followers, Some Honest Reasons Why

  • We weren't actually trying to promote the business through Facebook — we were using it as an educational channel, not a sales channel.
  • Facebook's organic reach for business pages is close to nothing without paid promotion, and paid promotion isn't cheap.
  • Posting often doesn't fix either of those two problems by itself.

What Actually Moved the Needle

  • Specific goals, not vague ones. "Grow the business" isn't measurable. "30 new clients in three months" is — you can tell whether it worked.
  • Trying things that might not work. Deciding something's too complicated before testing it just guarantees you learn nothing either way.
  • Asking for help. Professional advice, when it's actually needed, is cheaper than months of guessing.

Conclusion

None of this made our Facebook numbers impressive. What it did was give us an honest read on where our actual audience was — turns out, largely not there — which mattered more than any specific tactic. If you're running a small business's social presence yourself, the zero-follower phase isn't a sign you're doing it wrong; it's just the first data point.
