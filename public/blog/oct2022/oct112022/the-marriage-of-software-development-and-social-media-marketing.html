Introduction

Social media marketing and web development get treated as two separate line items — different teams, different budgets, sometimes different agencies entirely. But they're not actually parallel efforts. Social media's whole job is to move someone from a feed to your website. Whatever happens after that click is what determines whether the campaign meant anything.

What Social Media Actually Does

Done well, social gets you cost-effective reach to a specific audience and a channel for real-time customer service. Done honestly: it isn't free — someone's time runs the accounts and creates the content — and it isn't fast. Followers and trust build over months, not days. It's also not a replacement for the rest of your marketing; it's one channel among several, and its value depends entirely on what it's pointed at.

Where It's Actually Decided

A click from social is a person who was interested enough to leave the platform. What they find next is the whole game: does the page load fast, is it obvious what to do, does the content answer the reason they clicked in the first place? A slow or confusing landing page doesn't just lose that one visitor — it makes every dollar and hour spent on the post that sent them there a wash. This is the same math behind what we found auditing our own site's speed: the traffic doesn't matter if the destination can't hold onto it.

Search engines apply a version of the same logic. Google's ranking guidance keeps coming back to the same basics — pages that load quickly, layouts that aren't cluttered, content that matches what someone actually searched for. That's not a coincidence. A slow, cluttered site fails social traffic and search traffic for the same underlying reason: it wastes the attention it worked to earn.

Conclusion

Getting someone to click is the easy half. Website development is what determines whether that click was worth anything — and it's the half most social strategies never budget for.
