What a CRM is actually for

A CRM organizes contacts, tracks sales activity, and stores customer information in one place. As the number of channels a company uses to reach people grows, that consolidation matters more — without it, tracking where a customer actually came from and what they've done since becomes guesswork.

Where it helps marketing

Done well, a CRM gives marketing real insight into customer behavior and preferences, a full view of the relationship over time, and the ability to automate routine follow-up instead of doing it by hand. That's not nothing — it's the foundation personalized marketing is supposed to run on.

Where it breaks down

The gap shows up when customer data lives in five places instead of one — email tool, ad platform, sales system, support desk — none of them talking to each other. Getting a complete customer profile in that situation means piecing it together by hand, which nobody actually does consistently. The result is the exact opposite of personalization: missed communications, delayed responses, and messaging that doesn't reflect what the customer actually told you last time.

Choosing (and connecting) the right one

CRMs have gotten more capable over 25 years, but capability isn't the deciding factor — integration is. A CRM that consolidates data but doesn't connect to your email and ad platforms just creates one more silo instead of removing them. That's the part Taliferro focuses on: not picking a CRM off a features list, but making sure the systems around it actually share one customer view instead of five partial ones.
