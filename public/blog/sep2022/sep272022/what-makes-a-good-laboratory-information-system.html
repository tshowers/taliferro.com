A Good LIS Is Judged by What It Connects To

A lab system that can't talk to your EHR, your billing software, or the instruments on the bench doesn't remove manual work — it just relocates it to whoever's stuck copying data between systems by hand. Taliferro builds lab information systems around that principle: the system's value is mostly in what it connects to, not what it stores alone.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Sep 27, 2022 · 2 min read

Introduction

A good Laboratory Information System does more than store results. It organizes the lab's workflow, manages sample results, and generates patient and billing documents — and it does all of that through an interface people actually want to use. The best LIS platforms aren't standalone software; they're modular, built to plug into whatever else the lab already runs.

Workflow Management

The system needs to handle the entire workflow — sample management, order management, and the full lifecycle of a test result from registration and validation through storage and analysis. It should also organize the patient documents tied to each specimen, like health questionnaires or intake forms. If billing is part of the job, it needs to live inside the LIS, not in a second piece of software someone has to reconcile against the first.

An Interface People Don't Fight

A good LIS is easy to navigate on a computer, a tablet, or a phone, and it gets people to the data they need without digging through layers of menus or waiting on a report from a disconnected system. If staff need a workaround to get their job done, the interface has already failed.

Integration Is the Real Differentiator

A lab rarely runs on just an LIS. There's email for order notifications and invoices, a CRM for customer and provider relationships, instruments generating raw results, and often an inventory system for ordering test supplies. A good LIS connects to all of it through its API instead of asking staff to bridge the gap manually — that's the difference between a system that removes work and one that just moves it somewhere less visible.

Key Qualities of a Good Laboratory Information System (LIS)

Put together, that means evaluating an LIS on:

  • Reliability and uptime: The system must be stable, predictable, and resilient under real lab workloads.
  • Integration: A good LIS connects smoothly with instruments, EHRs, billing, and reporting systems instead of forcing manual workarounds.
  • Regulatory compliance: Support for CLIA, CAP, HIPAA, and other regulatory requirements should be built into workflows and audit trails.
  • Usability for lab staff: Screens, forms, and workflows should match how technologists and clinicians actually work, not how the software prefers.
  • Configurable rules and workflows: The LIS should adapt to different test menus, specimen types, and routing rules without custom code for every change.
  • Reporting and analytics: Access to clear, timely reports for quality, turnaround times, and operational performance.
  • Vendor support: Responsive, knowledgeable support and a roadmap that shows the product is actively maintained and improved.

Conclusion

When an LIS checks those boxes, it becomes a reliable partner in the lab rather than another system staff have to fight with — and that partnership, more than any single feature, is what makes a Laboratory Information System good.
