Helping My Client Get Promoted Is Part of the Job

Thirty years into IT consulting, the engagements people remember longest usually aren't the project itself — they're the moment someone helped them look good in front of their own boss. That's not scope creep. It's how the relationship actually gets built.

By Tyrone Showers

Co-Founder Taliferro

Article

Introduction

Most people want the same basic things at work: recognition, more money, and to be seen as genuinely good at what they do. When a client I'm working with wants that too, helping them get there isn't a distraction from the engagement — it's one of the more durable things I can do for the relationship.

Give Them Real Work to Point To

The simplest way to help someone toward a promotion is to put them on work that actually stretches them — something they're genuinely engaged in that lets their skills show. Knowing where they want to end up makes it easier to steer the assignments that get them there, so the case for a promotion is already written into what they've shipped by the time anyone asks.

Build the Case Before You Need It

If a client is missing a skill their next role requires, help them close that gap before the conversation happens, not during it. Then get specific about what actually justifies the promotion — a shipped result, a measurable improvement, a skill that's now clearly above their current level. "They're loyal and hardworking" doesn't move a promotion decision. A concrete, attributable outcome does.

Advocate Specifically, Not Generally

"I want them to get promoted" is a wish. "I want them promoted to X by this date, and here's the case" is a plan someone can actually act on. Agree with the client up front on what success looks like, so you're both working toward the same target — and if it doesn't happen on the timeline you hoped, that's not the end of it. There's always a next opportunity, and the groundwork you already laid makes it faster the second time.

Help Them Build Real Connections

Networking is obvious advice and still hard to act on. If a client hasn't built the habit, two things actually move the needle: framing it as more than a transaction — it's how knowledge and future opportunities reach them — and pushing them toward a network that isn't limited to their own specialty, so they have somewhere to turn when a problem shows up outside their usual scope.

Be Honest About the Odds

Before any of the above, tell them the truth about their actual chances. If the opportunity is real, say so. If it isn't — or if they don't see a path that exists — say that too. A client walking into a promotion conversation with accurate information does better than one walking in optimistic and unprepared, and it's better for the relationship either way.

Conclusion

None of this is complicated, but it takes real time: give them work worth pointing to, build the case in advance, ask for something specific, help them build a real network, and tell them the truth. It's not part of any invoice, but over thirty years it's been one of the more reliable ways a client turns into someone who calls Taliferro back for the next problem, too.
