Trying to Be Everywhere Online Is How You Disappear

A small business can post on every platform, run ads on all of them, and still get lost, because attention split five ways adds up to no attention anywhere. Taliferro isn't a social media marketing company — we build the systems that help a business pick the one or two channels worth showing up on and actually track whether it's working. Being everywhere isn't a strategy. It's how you disappear in plain sight.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Sep 4, 2022 · 2 min read

When the deeper issue is stalled execution, system design that removes drag shows how Taliferro turns execution work into working execution, and the momentum system keeps the work tied to outcomes instead of activity.

What we're not

People assume Taliferro runs social media accounts because we talk about marketing. We don't. What we actually build is the system underneath — the part that decides which channel deserves the budget, tracks whether a campaign is actually producing leads, and stops a business from spreading itself across five platforms it doesn't have time to manage well.

That distinction matters because most of the damage in small business marketing doesn't come from picking the wrong platform. It comes from trying to be present on all of them at once.

Start small and be realistic

Competing with established brands on their budget is a losing game if you're new to the market — so don't play it. Focus on doing one channel well instead of five channels badly. And be honest about your budget: an agency asking for $10K a month in ad spend isn't the right fit for a business with $500 to work with, no matter how good their pitch is.

Cheaper, more targeted options exist — Facebook and Instagram ads generally cost less per result than Google Ads for a small budget, especially early on when you're still learning what messaging actually lands. Results take time either way. The businesses that stick with one channel long enough to learn it usually beat the ones chasing the next platform.

Social media is a tool, not a strategy

Used well, a platform like Facebook lets you target a specific audience precisely — age, interest, location — instead of shouting into a general feed. That precision is genuinely useful for generating real leads. But precision only pays off if someone is tracking what's actually converting, because most of the cost in online marketing isn't the ad spend. It's the hours spent managing tools that never got compared against each other to see which one was worth keeping.

That's the part worth outsourcing or systematizing first — not the posting, the measuring. Know which channel is earning its keep before you add a second one.
