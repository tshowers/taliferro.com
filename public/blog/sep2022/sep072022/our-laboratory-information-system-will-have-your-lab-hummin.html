Your Lab Shouldn't Need a Meeting to Find a Result

A lab can be excellent at the science and still lose hours a week to paperwork, misplaced results, and systems that don't talk to each other. Taliferro built its Laboratory Information System to fix the operational side specifically — one place for results, HIPAA-compliant by default, and built to integrate with the records and billing systems a lab already uses. The lab's job is the science. The software's job is making sure nothing about running it gets in the way.

What a Laboratory Information System actually does

A Laboratory Information System (LIS) automates the workflow around lab results — logging, tracking, and reporting — so results don't live across a dozen spreadsheets and paper files. Plenty of general healthcare software touches labs; far fewer are actually built for how a lab operates day to day.

The real cost of skipping this isn't abstract. It's not having time for a sit-down meeting because you're still chasing down where a result went. It's a manager who can't see what's happening across the lab because it's split across three systems that don't talk to each other. A unified LIS fixes that directly: patients can check results on their own time, technicians see what's been run and what's next, and managers get one view instead of three.

What to actually look for

Picking a LIS comes down to a short list of real questions: does it scale with the lab, does it integrate with the billing and records systems already in use, and is it actually HIPAA compliant — not just described that way in a sales deck. Staff usability matters just as much as the feature list; a system nobody wants to use gets worked around, not adopted.

How our LIS handles it

  • Less manual entry. Fewer manual steps means fewer chances for a result to get entered wrong or missed entirely.
  • Result tracking that holds up. Every test is trackable by anyone authorized, from anywhere, with trend analysis across tests built in.
  • Reporting that spans time and sites. Longitudinal reports pull from multiple labs and visits automatically instead of being stitched together by hand.
  • Cloud infrastructure on Google Cloud Platform, built for HIPAA-compliant handling of electronically protected health information, with regular backups.
  • An interface that doesn't require training to enter, track, and report a sample — built for people with minimal lab-software experience, not just power users.

It's built for small to medium labs specifically, scaling to hundreds of users without the cost or complexity of an enterprise rollout, and it integrates with existing EMR and billing systems instead of asking a lab to replace what already works. Security follows the same standards that matter in healthcare — HIPAA, and frameworks like ISO/IEC 27001 and NIST 800-171 — because a lab's job is the science, not defending its own data.
