The mess behind a good-looking email

The first time I ran a campaign, I forgot half my audience, misspelled names, sent follow-ups to the wrong people, and wrote copy that never connected. Then I had no idea who to follow up with or when. The data underneath it was a mess — duplicates, missing info, bad email addresses. Total chaos.

Email campaigns aren't hard because of the writing. They're hard because of the work: the admin, the prep, the tracking. I learned that the hard way:

  • Timing matters more than tone.
  • Good contact data is everything — the best copy can't fix a wrong email address.
  • Tracking replies is only half the battle. Knowing what to do about them is the other half.
  • Messy or repeated outreach burns credibility fast.

Even when the email itself looked good, I was still behind — late follow-ups, forgotten warm leads, no easy way to connect what I'd sent to what should happen next.

I stopped doing it all myself

Eventually I hit a wall. I couldn't keep running campaigns and still find time to actually talk to people. So Taliferro built TODD to take the parts I used to dread: it drafts emails based on tone, context, and where the conversation left off; validates contacts before I waste a send; tracks opens, replies, and click-throughs; and flags bounced emails and bad domains before they become a pattern I have to notice on my own.

What actually changed

Campaigns stopped being a big event and became a rhythm. The three things that matter most if you're starting out: don't send on unclean data, don't blast everyone the same message, and don't stop after one email — the value is in the follow-through, not the first send.

Every missed follow-up is a missed chance. If you're serious about outreach, get serious about the system underneath it, not just the words. That's the part that actually made the difference.
