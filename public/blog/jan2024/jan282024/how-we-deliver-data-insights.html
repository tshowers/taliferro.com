A Chart Is Not an Insight It's Homework You Haven't Done Yet

A bar chart showing sales dropped in March is data, beautifully rendered. It is not, on its own, useful — nobody's business improves because a graph looks nice. The insight is the sentence after the chart: this dropped because of X, and here's what to do about it. Taliferro doesn't measure our data work by how many dashboards get built. We measure it by how many of those charts actually produced a decision someone made.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Jan 28, 2024 · 4 min read

Chart vs. Insight

Ask most vendors what "data insights" means and they'll show you a dashboard. Dashboards are useful. They're also frequently the end product delivered, when the actual deliverable a business needed was a decision. A chart that sits in a shared drive, admired once and never acted on, has produced zero business value regardless of how well-designed it is.

Our Approach

  • Data collection and integration: gathering data from wherever it actually lives — databases, spreadsheets, real-time streams — into one place that can actually be analyzed.
  • Cleaning and preprocessing: raw data is messy. Inconsistencies and missing values get resolved before analysis, not discovered after a decision was already made on bad numbers.
  • Analysis and visualization: turning cleaned data into a chart that makes the pattern visible. Below is a simple example — grouping raw sales data by month and rendering it as a bar chart:
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

# Load data
data = pd.read_csv('sales_data.csv')

# Group data by month
monthly_sales = data.groupby('Month')['Sales'].sum()

# Create a bar chart
plt.figure(figsize=(10, 6))
plt.bar(monthly_sales.index, monthly_sales.values)
plt.xlabel('Month')
plt.ylabel('Sales')
plt.title('Monthly Sales Analysis')
plt.xticks(rotation=45)
plt.show()

This produces a chart. It does not, by itself, produce an insight — that requires someone to look at the chart and answer "so what."

  • Interpretation is the actual deliverable. A chart showing a March dip in sales is a fact. "March dropped because a shipping delay pushed orders into April" is an insight — the same fact, plus the cause, plus what to do differently next March.
  • Recommendations that a business can act on this week. A report that ends in "continue monitoring trends" hasn't actually recommended anything. One that ends in "reorder SKU 4471 two weeks earlier" has.

What This Actually Changes

  • Decisions instead of guesses: a specific recommendation replaces intuition, but only if someone actually acts on it.
  • Real efficiency gains: insight that points at a specific bottleneck is what gets fixed — a dashboard nobody reads fixes nothing.
  • Faster response to change: a business that can see a shift in the data early can react before a competitor does.
  • Better-targeted product decisions: understanding actual customer behavior, not assumed behavior, is what makes a roadmap decision defensible.
