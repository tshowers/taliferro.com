Most "Custom Software" Is a Template Wearing Your Logo

A vendor can call almost anything "custom" — the truth is closer to this: 80% of any SaaS product is the same problems everyone else already solved, like authentication, billing, and notifications, and the last 20% is the part that actually makes the business different. Taliferro builds the boring 80% fast, using proven patterns, specifically so we can spend real time on the 20% that's actually unique to the client. The 80/20 Problem

Every SaaS product needs a login screen, a billing system, a way to send a password reset email, and a dozen other things that have already been solved correctly thousands of times. None of that is where a business's actual value lives. The value lives in the specific workflow, the specific calculation, the specific thing this business does that its competitors don't. A vendor who treats the whole build as equally "custom" spends the client's budget reinventing a password reset flow instead of perfecting the one feature that's actually the point.

Our Approach

Taliferro's process starts with understanding not just what a client wants built, but why — which parts of the request are genuinely novel and which are standard problems wearing a custom label. The standard parts get built fast, using proven, well-tested patterns, because reinventing them adds risk and cost without adding value. The genuinely unique parts get the deep research, design attention, and iteration that custom work is supposed to mean in the first place.

What This Actually Buys a Client

  • Faster time-to-market: not rebuilding solved problems means the unique parts ship sooner, not later.
  • Fewer bugs in the boring parts: proven patterns for auth, billing, and notifications have already had their edge cases found by someone else.
  • More budget for what matters: money not spent reinventing a login flow is money spent on the feature that's actually the product's reason to exist.
  • Real scalability: the parts of the system built on proven patterns tend to scale predictably, because they're not the first attempt at solving that particular problem.

Conclusion

Entrepreneurs don't need everything built from scratch — they need the parts that matter built well, and the parts that don't matter built fast and reliably. Taliferro's custom SaaS work draws that line deliberately on every project, instead of charging a client for "custom" work on problems that were already solved a decade ago.
