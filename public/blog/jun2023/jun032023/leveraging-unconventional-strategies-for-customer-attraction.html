Stop Treating Pinterest Like Instagram's Little Cousin

Most social media advice defaults to Instagram and X. Two channels that get skipped — Pinterest as a blog-traffic engine, and short-form video on YouTube and TikTok — often outperform them for actual customer acquisition. Taliferro breaks down why each one works and how to use it.

TS Tyrone Showers Co-Founder Taliferro · Jun 3, 2023 · 2 min read

Introduction

Most social media strategy conversations start and end with Instagram and X. Two channels that rarely come up — Pinterest as a discovery engine for existing blog content, and short-form video on YouTube and TikTok — routinely outperform them for actual customer acquisition, because they work with how people search and browse instead of against it.

Pinterest: Turning a Blog Post Into a Search Result

Pinterest functions less like a social network and more like a visual search engine — people use it to find ideas, and pins keep surfacing in search for months or years after posting, unlike a tweet or a feed post that's dead within a day. A blog post turned into a well-designed pin graphic, with a clear title and a description built around the keywords someone would actually search, becomes a standing traffic source instead of a one-time announcement.

The mechanics matter: rich pins pull metadata directly from the blog post, so the pin stays in sync with the content instead of going stale. That's what makes Pinterest worth the setup time for content-heavy sites — it turns existing blog posts into a compounding traffic channel without writing anything new.

YouTube and TikTok: Different Platforms, Different Jobs

YouTube rewards depth. A video that actually teaches something — solves a real problem, answers a question people are searching for — builds authority in a way a blog post alone doesn't, because seeing someone explain something builds more trust than reading it. Consistent branding and keyword-rich titles and descriptions are what make that content findable months after publishing, the same way a good blog post keeps ranking.

TikTok rewards something almost opposite: speed and authenticity over production value. It's built for short, immediate content, and it disproportionately reaches a younger, more digitally native audience that tunes out anything that looks like a traditional ad. Businesses that do well there participate in the platform's actual culture — trends, sounds, format conventions — rather than repurposing a polished commercial.

Both channels reward consistency more than any single viral hit. A single video reaching a million views once is a fluke; a channel publishing on a reliable cadence is what actually compounds into an audience.

Conclusion

Pinterest, YouTube, and TikTok aren't unconventional because they're obscure — they're unconventional because most marketing strategies default to the platforms everyone already thinks of first. Treating a blog as raw material for Pinterest, and treating video as its own medium instead of a repurposed ad, is what actually unlocks the reach these three channels offer.
