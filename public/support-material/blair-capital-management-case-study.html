Client Background

A respected firm with no digital footprint

Blair Capital Management is a financial asset management firm based in Fox Lake, Illinois. The firm managed client portfolios and had established relationships, but had built the business entirely offline. There was no website. Prospective investors had no way to research the firm or view performance data before making a commitment.

The back office ran on spreadsheets. Client data was fragmented across files, emails, and manual records. Onboarding new clients required significant manual work from staff at every step. None of it was connected.

The firm knew the gap was costing them. Institutional credibility in financial services increasingly starts with digital presence, and Blair Capital had none. They came to Taliferro needing the full build — not just a website, but the infrastructure underneath it.

Challenges

Starting from scratch in a trust-dependent industry

Financial services clients evaluate firms differently than other industries. Performance data, security, and professional presentation all carry weight before any conversation begins.

  1. No digital presence whatsoever. Blair Capital had no website, no investor portal, and no online channel for prospective clients to find or evaluate the firm.
  2. Investors couldn't see performance data. Portfolio yield figures existed internally but there was no way to surface them to clients or prospects online. Reporting required manual preparation for each client.
  3. Data fragmented across manual systems. Client information, portfolio records, and communication history lived in disconnected spreadsheets and email threads — not in any unified system.
  4. Onboarding was entirely manual. Each new client required extensive staff time to gather information, verify details, and set up accounts. There was no automation and no integration between steps.
  5. Infrastructure needed to be financial-grade. Security, availability, and compliance requirements for financial services ruled out generic hosting. Cloud architecture needed to be purposefully designed.
Approach

Website, APIs, and cloud — built as one system, not three projects

Taliferro scoped the engagement as an integrated build. The website, the data layer, and the infrastructure were designed together so they would perform as one system rather than bolt-on additions.

Web Development

First professional website

Taliferro designed and built Blair Capital's first website — professional, fast, and built to convert prospective investors into inquiries. The site established credibility, communicated the firm's strategy, and gave clients a place to log in and view their accounts.

  • Mobile-first, performance-optimized build.
  • Investor login portal with account views.
  • Clear messaging for prospective clients.
API Design

Real-time yield data via APIs

The most visible outcome of the engagement was investors being able to see live portfolio performance for the first time. Taliferro designed and built APIs that pulled real-time YTD yield data and surfaced it on the client portal — replacing manual PDF reports delivered on a delay.

  • Real-time YTD yield data on the investor portal.
  • Eliminated manual performance reporting.
  • Secure, authenticated data access for each client.
Cloud Architecture

GCP infrastructure built for financial services

The full stack was deployed on Google Cloud Platform. Taliferro designed the architecture for availability, security, and compliance — appropriate for a firm handling client financial data. Infrastructure was configured for scale from day one.

  • GCP deployment with financial-grade security controls.
  • Scalable architecture designed to grow with AUM.
  • Reduced infrastructure overhead vs. on-premises options.
Data Integration

Fragmented data centralized

Client records, portfolio data, and operational information that had lived in spreadsheets and disconnected systems were migrated and integrated into the new platform. Onboarding workflows were automated, replacing the manual steps that had consumed staff time.

  • Single source of truth for client and portfolio data.
  • Automated intake and onboarding workflows.
  • 60% reduction in onboarding time.
The Turning Point

When investors could see performance data, the conversation changed

Before the API build, every performance conversation required staff to pull data manually and prepare a report. After launch, investors could log in and see real-time yield figures. That change — data available on demand rather than on request — directly drove the 80% engagement increase and contributed to the AUM growth trajectory.

Infrastructure Impact

50% cost reduction came from the architecture, not just the tools

Moving from manual processes and fragmented tooling to an integrated GCP stack eliminated redundant work at every level. Reporting, onboarding, data management — all of it became faster and cheaper once the infrastructure was designed to support it rather than work around it.

Lessons Learned

Digital infrastructure in financial services is a competitive asset, not a compliance checkbox

The Blair Capital engagement is a clear example of what happens when a firm decides to treat its digital presence as a growth driver.

  1. Credibility starts before the first conversation. Prospective investors evaluate firms online before reaching out. A professional web presence with live performance data closes the gap between interest and inquiry.
  2. Real-time data access changes client behavior. Moving from delayed manual reports to live API-driven dashboards directly increased engagement. Clients checked their portfolios more often and reached out with sharper, more informed questions.
  3. Build the system, not just the website. A website without the data layer underneath it would not have produced these results. The API design and cloud architecture were equally load-bearing parts of the outcome.
  4. Cloud infrastructure compounds over time. The 50% cost reduction was partly immediate — eliminating manual processes — and partly structural. A scalable GCP architecture means cost-per-client decreases as AUM grows, not increases.
  5. Onboarding speed signals operational quality. Faster onboarding is not just an efficiency win. For prospective clients evaluating multiple firms, a smooth, fast intake process is itself a trust signal.
Bottom Line

300% AUM growth from building the right infrastructure

Blair Capital did not need a marketing campaign. They needed a credible digital presence backed by real data. Taliferro built the website, the APIs, and the cloud architecture — and the business results followed within 12 months.
