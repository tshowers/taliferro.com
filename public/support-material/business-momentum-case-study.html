Client Background

A strong reputation was not enough to make the work sustainable

A Seattle-based fireplace repair company had built its reputation the right way: years of quality work, strong customer relationships, and glowing Yelp reviews. Referrals kept business steady, and customers trusted the owners because they cared about the craft.

But behind the scenes, the business was wearing them down. They answered calls, scheduled jobs, created estimates, sent invoices, chased payments, and followed up with customers. The repair work was only part of the job. The administrative work was becoming its own full-time business.

Their website acted like a digital business card. Visitors could find a phone number and email address, but they could not book a service, request a quote, or pay online. Every customer interaction required a call or email, which created missed opportunities when the owners were in the field.

The owners were not trying to grow for the sake of growth. They had even talked about retiring early because the workload had become too much. Hiring was not a clean answer either. Skilled workers who met their standards were hard to find.

Challenges

The business was successful, but the system around it was heavy

The owners had demand, trust, and steady work. What they did not have was a system that protected their time.

  1. Limited workforce and hiring struggles The company was running near capacity with a small team. Hiring additional skilled workers was difficult because the available labor pool did not meet the owners' quality standards.
  2. Time-consuming manual work Calls, scheduling, estimates, invoices, follow-ups, and customer service all sat on the owners' shoulders. They spent too much time managing the business instead of doing the work customers paid them to do.
  3. Missed opportunities from slow response times Customers had to call or email to move forward. When the owners were on job sites, inquiries waited. Some prospects moved on before anyone could respond.
  4. Fear that growth would create more work The owners believed more jobs would mean more stress, more scheduling, more paperwork, and more people to manage. To them, technology sounded like another burden instead of a way out.
Approach

TODD turned the website into a working system

The goal was not to overhaul the business. The goal was to remove repetitive tasks from the owners' day without forcing them to change the quality of their work.

Scheduling

Customers could book without calling

Before TODD, customers had to call or email, wait for a response, then go back and forth to find an appointment. With TODD, customers could choose from available times online.

  • Reduced missed opportunities.
  • Removed unnecessary calls.
  • Protected the owners' time in the field.
Quotes

Estimates moved faster

Before TODD, every estimate had to be created manually. With TODD, customers could submit job details online and receive faster quote guidance.

  • Fewer delays.
  • Less back-and-forth.
  • More serious inquiries.
Payments

Invoicing stopped eating time

Before TODD, invoices and late-payment follow-ups required manual effort. With TODD, invoice generation and payment reminders became part of the workflow.

  • More consistent cash flow.
  • Less payment chasing.
  • Cleaner closeout after each job.
Follow-Up

Reviews no longer depended on memory

Before TODD, follow-ups were inconsistent and satisfied customers were not always asked for reviews. TODD sent follow-up messages and review requests after service.

  • Stronger post-service engagement.
  • More review opportunities.
  • Better online visibility without extra work.
Website Shift

The website stopped being a placeholder

The old site only proved the company existed. TODD made the website behave more like a digital employee: booking, quoting, collecting payments, and guiding customers forward.

Operational Shift

The owners got fewer interruptions and better control

The company did not need a larger team to reduce pressure. It needed a better operating layer around the work the owners already did well.

Lessons Learned

Automation was not about getting bigger. It was about getting time back

The owners discovered that the right system does not replace the craft. It removes the admin work around the craft.

  1. Automation does not have to mean more work. The owners feared technology would add complexity. Instead, TODD removed the tasks that kept interrupting their day.
  2. A website can be a business tool, not just a digital card. The site moved from static contact information to booking, quote support, payment support, and customer management.
  3. Scaling does not always require hiring. The company completed more jobs and increased revenue by automating the administrative layer instead of adding staff first.
  4. Customers expect convenience. People are used to booking services online. If they cannot take the next step quickly, they may choose another provider.
  5. The right system gives owners control over their time. Before TODD, the owners were always reacting. After TODD, more of the routine work happened without them needing to touch every step.
Bottom Line

TODD gave them a third option

Before TODD, the owners felt trapped between staying small, hiring before they were ready, or working longer hours. TODD created another path: let the system handle the work that drained their time.

Conclusion

The business finally had breathing room

Technology did not force them to change the quality of their work. It removed the tasks that kept getting in the way of that work. They could take on more jobs without more stress, spend more time away from work without worrying about missed calls, and focus on what they did best: fixing fireplaces, not paperwork.
