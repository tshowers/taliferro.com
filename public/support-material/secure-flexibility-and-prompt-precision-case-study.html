Client Background

The agency wanted AI, but not at the cost of data integrity

A large state agency responsible for workforce development, education funding, and policy enforcement needed AI assistance across multiple departments. Legal handled confidential content. Finance managed sensitive budget forecasts. Outreach worked with protected community data. HR had its own workforce-related records and workflows.

Leadership wanted all departments to benefit from AI-enhanced workflows, but data could not move freely across units. The agency needed a system that could respect department boundaries while still giving teams practical support for writing, review, analysis, and daily work.

Off-the-shelf AI tools were not enough. The agency needed department-aware governance, permission-based prompting, prompt testing, and a way to improve output quality without forcing sensitive teams into one shared AI environment.

Challenges

The agency could not treat every department like one shared workspace

Each department needed AI support, but each department had different data rules, workflows, and risk boundaries.

  1. Strict data silos had to stay intact. Legal, Finance, Outreach, and HR operated in isolation by policy, confidentiality requirement, and operational necessity.
  2. General-purpose AI tools lacked hard separation. Shared-suite tools did not provide the level of structural control the agency needed for department-specific AI use.
  3. Prompt output was not reliable enough. Staff spent too much time refining vague, inaccurate, or non-actionable AI responses.
  4. Departments needed customization. One-size-fits-all AI could not reflect department vocabulary, policies, files, datasets, and task patterns.
Approach

TODD was configured for secure department-aware AI

Taliferro deployed TODD with architecture and prompting controls designed for siloed environments.

Containers

Each department received its own secure container

Department containers protected data boundaries and allowed each unit to operate independently or collaborate only when allowed by admin settings.

  • No cross-department data bleed.
  • Isolated or shared modes by policy.
  • Department-specific retrieval and storage rules.
RBAC

Prompts respected role-based access

Role-based access control scoped prompts, files, datasets, and policies to the user’s department and permissions.

  • Legal saw Legal context only.
  • Finance remained protected.
  • Admins controlled source visibility.
Prompt Tools

Prompting became easier to learn and repeat

TODD included reusable prompt templates, prompt history analysis, testing support, and auto-tuning based on successful requests.

  • Templates reduced guesswork.
  • History analysis improved revisions.
  • Testing avoided risky production prompts.
Governance

Rules could change without new software

The agency could update visibility settings, collaboration rules, and container-level governance as department needs evolved.

  • Flexible rules.
  • Department-level control.
  • Better long-term maintainability.
Prompt Precision

TODD did not leave users guessing

The system taught better prompting through templates, testing, prompt history, and successful output patterns. Users learned to ask for specific output types, reference the right files or datasets, and avoid overloading one prompt with too many tasks.

Department Fit

Each unit used AI differently

Legal used isolation for sensitive content. HR generated onboarding documents faster. Finance used Consistent Output Protocol for repeatable budget summaries. Outreach collaborated with HR when hiring campaigns required shared planning.

Lessons Learned

Secure AI depends on architecture and user behavior

The deployment showed that privacy, governance, and prompt quality must work together.

  1. Data separation has to be structural. Sensitive departments need more than policy reminders. They need architecture that prevents data from crossing boundaries.
  2. Prompting needs product support. Templates, examples, testing, and revision guidance help users get better results faster.
  3. Specific prompts produce better outputs. Requests such as “write a two-paragraph executive summary” create more usable results than vague instructions.
  4. Context references improve grounding. File names, policy IDs, and dataset references help the system produce more relevant answers.
  5. Governance needs flexibility. Departments may need isolation one day and controlled collaboration the next. The system should support both.
Bottom Line

The agency did not have to choose between AI and privacy

TODD delivered department-aware AI with infrastructure-level separation, container governance, permission-based prompting, and tools that improved output quality.

Conclusion

TODD was built not just to answer questions, but to respect boundaries, teach better prompting, and evolve with the people and departments using it.
