The real problem

When Agile stalls, delivery gets loud

Agile rarely fails because a team picked Scrum instead of Kanban. It fails because the same three questions never get answered fast enough: who owns this, is it actually ready to start, and what happens after someone says "let's do it" in a meeting.

  • Meetings replace decisions. A meeting ends with "let's do X." Two weeks later, nobody remembers agreeing to X, and it never happened.
  • Work starts before it's ready. A ticket gets pulled into the sprint with two vague sentences. The engineer spends the first two days just figuring out what it's actually asking for.
  • Dependencies hide in plain sight. Team A is waiting on Team B's API. Nobody wrote that down, so it looks like Team A is behind — they're actually just stuck.
  • The numbers stop meaning anything. Velocity goes up because tickets got split into smaller pieces, not because more real work shipped.

How we coach

A practical coaching loop your team can live with

Taliferro doesn't come in and relabel your meetings "Agile ceremonies." We watch how work actually moves through your team, from idea to shipped, and fix the specific points where it gets stuck.

1) Find where work gets stuck

We watch your team work for a sprint or two — what sits waiting, what gets reworked, where decisions stall. Most teams are surprised by where the actual delay is hiding.

Output: a one-page map of exactly what's slowing you down.

2) Make "ready" mean something

We write a short, specific checklist: a ticket doesn't enter the sprint until it passes it. No more starting work nobody has actually defined yet.

Output: a readiness checklist, built from your own backlog.

3) Fix the meetings that aren't working

Standup, planning, review, retro — each one gets a job. If a meeting doesn't end in a decision or a next step, we cut it or redesign it, and we make sure decisions get written down where the team can see them, so they don't just live in someone's memory.

Output: a meeting schedule your team can run without us in the room.

4) Coach it live, not from a slide deck

We sit in your real planning and standups for a few sprints and correct the pattern as it happens, instead of lecturing about theory in a workshop.

Output: a written playbook plus a coaching plan for next quarter.

FAQ

Common questions teams ask before they commit

We already “do Agile.” What would you change?

We don’t change labels. We change outcomes. We focus on readiness, ownership, decision points, and how work becomes finishable. If your standups are loud and delivery is slow, the system needs tightening.

Do you force Scrum or SAFe?

No. We coach Scrum, Kanban, and SAFe, but we don’t force process theater. We use the minimum structure needed to improve flow and delivery reliability.

How fast can we see impact?

Teams usually feel relief quickly when ownership and readiness become explicit. You can often stabilize cadence in a few weeks, then compound improvements over the next cycle.

Can you coach leadership and the team?

Yes. Agile stalls when leadership decisions drift or when the team can’t get clear direction. We align leadership on decision rules, then coach the team on the operating rhythm.
